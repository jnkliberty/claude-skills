# Hook Patterns & Ending Taxonomy

**Source:** David Arnoux, viralbrain.ai — 62,130 viral posts analyzed (January 2026)

---

## Hook Taxonomy with Win Rates

### Tier 1: Curiosity Gap (Default)

- **Avg engagement:** 6,280
- **Win rate:** 66%
- **Structure:** Create an information gap the reader must fill. Promise a payoff without revealing it.

**Research examples:**
```
Pizza orders have spiked around the pentagon...

you know what that means...
```

```
we 100x'd our organic reach (SEO/GEO) without writing blogs

here's the exact playbook we used...
```

### Tier 2: Emotional

- **Avg engagement:** 5,976
- **Win rate:** 65%
- **Structure:** Tap into a feeling the reader is already having. Empathy-first. The reader sees themselves before they see your point.

**Research examples:**
```
If you're feeling a bit sad this Christmas, I need to tell you something...
```

```
Nobody talks about how lonely it gets when you go solo.

Here's what I wish someone told me.
```

**When to use:** When the post is about a shared emotional experience — loneliness of freelancing, imposter syndrome, the fear before shipping something new. The hook validates the feeling, then the body provides the insight.

### Tier 3: Contrarian

- **Avg engagement:** 4,231
- **Win rate:** 50%
- **Structure:** Challenge conventional wisdom. State something most people believe, then flip it.

**Research examples:**
```
Everyone says "post consistently." They're wrong.

Here's what actually moves the needle.
```

```
The worst career advice I ever got: "Follow your passion."
```

**When to use:** When you have genuine evidence or experience that contradicts the mainstream. Don't be contrarian for shock value — back it up in the body. Example: "How do you get good with AI? Being lazy."

### Tier 4: Story Opener

- **Avg engagement:** 4,028
- **Win rate:** 41%
- **Structure:** Drop the reader into a scene. Start in medias res — no preamble, no context-setting. The reader lands in the middle of the action.

**Research examples:**
```
3 years ago, I was making $40K and hating every Monday.

Then I made one phone call.
```

```
Yesterday I said "everything broke."

12 hours later, it was the best deploy we've shipped.
```

**When to use:** When the post tells a transformation story. The opener drops you into the "before" — the body walks through the change — the ending lands on the "after."

### Tier 5: Bold Claim

- **Avg engagement:** 3,981
- **Win rate:** 52%
- **Structure:** Make a claim that demands proof. High risk, high reward. The reader stays to see if you can back it up.

**Research examples:**
```
I made $100K in 90 days with one skill.

No ads. No team. No luck.
```

```
Your LinkedIn profile is losing you money every day.
```

**When to use:** When you have hard proof — numbers, screenshots, specific results. Bold claims without substance get punished by BREW360. Julian's "Filed my LLC. Total cost: $425." is a bold claim backed by vivid specifics.

### Amplifiers (boost any hook type)

#### Proof of Work
- **Performance:** High (amplifies other hook types)
- **Structure:** Show exactly how you achieved a result. Specific numbers, tools, timelines.

#### Vivid Specifics
- **Performance:** High (amplifies other hook types)
- **Structure:** Include concrete details — dollar amounts, timeframes, tool names, locations.

**These are not standalone hook types** — they make any hook stronger. A curiosity gap with vivid specifics outperforms a bare curiosity gap. A story opener with proof of work outperforms a vague story opener.

### Question Hooks

- **Avg engagement:** 1,511
- **Win rate:** 24%
- **Status:** Viable but weaker. Only use when the question is truly provocative — something the reader can't scroll past without forming an answer.

**Example:** "What would you do if your biggest client fired you tomorrow?" works. "What's your favorite productivity tip?" doesn't.

### HOW TO Hooks

- **Avg engagement:** 2,785
- **Win rate:** 29%
- **Status:** Functional but not exciting. Use sparingly. The "how to" format signals utility, which can work for technical audiences, but it lacks the emotional pull of the top tiers.

### Dead Last: List-Promise

- **Avg engagement:** 28
- **Win rate:** 0%
- **Status:** Dead
- **Examples of what NOT to do:**
  - "7 ways to improve your LinkedIn posts"
  - "5 things you need to know about AI"
  - "10 mistakes every founder makes"
- **Why:** Overused. Reads as clickbait. BREW360 deprioritizes.

## The Two-Line Hook Formula

Every LinkedIn post opens with two lines separated by a blank line.

```
Line 1: STOP THE SCROLL
Emotional punch / contradiction / curiosity / shocking fact / surprising number

[BLANK LINE — this is critical]

Line 2: CURIOSITY GAP
What's surprising / revealed / the secret / the gap the reader needs to fill
```

### Why the blank line matters
- Creates visual breathing room
- Builds anticipation
- Improves scroll-stop rate
- Critical formatting element on mobile

## Two-Line Hook Templates

Use these patterns. Adapt the content.

### The legal crime
```
I committed tax fraud. Legally.

It cost $425 and a boring Thursday.
```

### The contradiction
```
[Counter-intuitive statement that sounds wrong]

[One-line setup that makes the reader need the explanation]
```

Example:
```
My best hire doesn't have a pulse.

It costs $0/month and works 24/7.
```

### The specific number + gap
```
[Surprising number]. [What it represents].

[The part the reader doesn't expect]
```

Example:
```
30 commits. Zero features. Every commit was just changing words.

Two days later, inbound doubled.
```

### The honest failure
```
[What went wrong in one blunt sentence]

[The part that makes it interesting]
```

Example:
```
3 hours debugging a deploy that never worked.

Then I gave up and it worked in 5 minutes.
```

### The before/after
```
[The before state — relatable, painful]

[The after state — surprising, specific]
```

### The callback + escalation
```
[Reference to previous post]

[What happened next that the reader didn't expect]
```

## Ending Patterns

### What works

#### Provocative statement
A bold claim that invites contradiction or agreement. The reader feels compelled to respond because they either agree strongly or disagree strongly.

**Julian examples (existing, strong):**
- "Polish is not a phase. It's a pass you make after every feature ships."
- "Words are positioning. Pick them on purpose."

**Julian examples (to use):**
- "The boring paperwork is the business. Everything else is just marketing."
- "If you're overthinking it, you're not building."

#### Specific ask
A concrete request. Not "what do you think?" but a specific action.

**Julian examples (existing):**
- "Happy to share it!"
- "Try it."
- "Go check."

**Patterns:**
- "Drop a DM if you want the [specific thing]."
- "Comment [keyword] and I'll send you [specific resource]."
- "Look at your [specific thing]. Does it [specific test]?"

#### Direct challenge
Challenge the reader to do something concrete.

**Julian examples (existing):**
- "If you're sitting on a business idea, the right time is a random Thursday with $425."
- "Look at the demo on your homepage. Does it show what you actually built?"

#### Comment-for-access CTA

Describe a high-value resource, ask users to comment a specific keyword, and offer to send the resource. This is the highest-performing ending pattern in the dataset — 4 of the top 10 viral posts use it. One post generated 5,286 comments with this pattern alone.

**Structure:**
```
[Describe the value — what you're offering and why it matters]

1. Connect with me
2. Comment "[KEYWORD]"
3. Repost for priority access
```

**Variations:**
- Add urgency: "Reply within 24 hours for [bonus]."
- Stack actions: Connect + Comment + Repost (more actions = more algorithmic signals)
- Keep the keyword short and memorable: "PLAYBOOK", "TEMPLATE", "GTM"

**Rules:**
- Must be paired with a **genuine, high-value resource.** Don't use this pattern for vapor. If you promise a playbook, have a playbook.
- Works best at the end of a post that already delivers value — the CTA feels like an extension of the value, not a bait-and-switch.
- Don't overuse. If every post ends with "Comment [keyword]", it loses impact. Reserve for posts where you genuinely have something to share.

### What's dead

#### Generic questions
| Pattern | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Thoughts?" | Dead | Engagement bait. BREW360 deprioritizes. |
| "What do you think?" | Dead | Too vague. No one feels compelled. |
| "Agree or disagree?" | Dead | Binary. Low-effort response. |
| "What would you do?" | Weak | Generic. No specificity. |

**Exception:** Specific questions with context still work.
- "What's your worst deploy debugging story?" — specific, relatable, invites a real response.
- "How did you figure out your rate?" — specific to a shared experience.

The difference: generic questions ask for nothing. Specific questions ask for a story.

## Transformation Arc

The best posts don't just tell a story — they show a **transformation**. A character faces a problem, goes through a specific change or realization, and arrives somewhere new.

### Structure

```
HOOK:  Creates the gap (where did this person start?)
BODY:  Walks through the transformation (what changed? what did they learn?)
ENDING: Resolves it (where did they land? what's the takeaway?)
```

### Why it works

The hook creates tension. The body builds through the middle — the struggle, the insight, the shift. The ending resolves it. This mirrors how we naturally process experiences.

100% of viral patterns in the dataset include personal stories. But the ones that hit hardest have a clear **before → shift → after** arc. Not just "here's what happened" but "here's who I was, what broke, and what I see now."

### How to apply

When writing a LinkedIn post with a personal story:
1. **Identify the transformation.** What changed? What did you believe before that you don't believe now?
2. **Start in the "before."** The hook should place the reader in the old state.
3. **Show the catalyst.** What specific moment or realization caused the shift?
4. **Land on the "after."** The ending should feel earned — not prescribed, but discovered.

The upgrade is making the transformation explicit. Not just "I did this thing" but "I was this person, this happened, and now I see it differently."

## Hook + Ending Pairing

The hook creates the gap. The ending resolves it and opens a new one.

**Strong pairing:**
- Hook: Curiosity gap → Ending: Provocative statement (resolves the curiosity, leaves a new idea)
- Hook: Contradiction → Ending: Direct challenge (proves the contradiction, dares the reader)
- Hook: Honest failure → Ending: One-line lesson (shows the growth, drops the principle)
- Hook: Story opener → Ending: Comment-for-access CTA (completes the story, offers deeper value)
- Hook: Emotional → Ending: Provocative statement (validates the feeling, reframes it)

**Weak pairing:**
- Hook: Anything → Ending: "Thoughts?" (wastes the momentum)
- Hook: Curiosity gap → Ending: No resolution (reader feels cheated)
- Hook: Bold claim → Ending: No proof (reader feels manipulated)

## Anti-Patterns Summary

| Pattern | Data | Action |
|---|---|---|
| List-promise hooks ("7 ways to...") | 28 avg, 0% win rate | Never use |
| Generic ending questions | Low engagement | Use provocative statements or CTAs instead |
| No blank line in hook | Reduces scroll-stop rate | Always include blank line |
| Hook without specifics | Underperforms | Add numbers, tools, timelines |
| Hook without proof of work | Underperforms | Show the "how" not just the "what" |
| Wall of text after hook | 71% worse | Break into 14+ short paragraphs |
| Bold claim without evidence | Punished by BREW360 | Always back up claims in the body |
| Comment CTA without real resource | Erodes trust | Only use when you have genuine value to share |
